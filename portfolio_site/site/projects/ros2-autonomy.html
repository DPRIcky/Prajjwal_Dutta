Three Halves of an Autonomous Robot

A mobile robot has to answer three questions in a loop: what's out there, where am I, and how do I get there without wasting energy. This is a ROS 2 stack that answers each one from scratch — no nav2, no pcl_ros, no amcl — built as three independent packages against the same simulated cave world. Every algorithm below is hand-implemented: RANSAC on point normals, a histogram filter over half a million belief cells, A* with Bresenham line-of-sight pruning.

The Stack

Pick one.

Each package stands alone with its own repository, its own simulation setup, and its own results. They're presented in the order a robot actually uses them.

01 / Perception

OAK-D Cylinder Perception Pipeline→

RGB-D point clouds into labeled objects across ten stages: box filter, voxel downsample, k-NN + SVD normals, RANSAC plane removal, Euclidean clustering, cylinder fitting from normal cross products, and HSV semantic labeling — with every stage published as its own debug cloud.

02 / Localization

3D Discrete Bayes Filter Localization→

A histogram filter holding the full posterior over (x, y, θ) as an 80×80×72 array, predicted by Turn-Go-Turn odometry decomposition with Gaussian diffusion and corrected by range–bearing landmark likelihoods — converging within 0.6 m after two or three sightings, and failing instructively when kidnapped.

03 / Planning

Energy-Aware A* Motion Planning→

A* on an intelligently downsampled 80×80 grid, greedy pruning with Bresenham line-of-sight, diagonal corner checks, and a Turn-Go-Turn controller tuned for energy rather than speed — 67× faster planning, 89.8% fewer waypoints, ~90% energy saved, and 34 unit tests proving each layer.

Common Ground

One world, three problems.

Localization and planning share the same testbed — a 16 × 16 m cave in the Stage simulator, the same 0.2 m grid resolution, and the same Turn-Go-Turn motion decomposition, which appears in one package as a motion model for prediction and in the other as a controller for execution. That symmetry is the most useful thing these three projects taught together: the same decomposition of movement serves both to guess where you ended up and to decide how to get somewhere.

The perception package works in sensor space instead — OAK-D RGB-D frames replayed from recorded rosbags — but shares the discipline: publish the intermediate state, tune from a single config, and verify against something other than a screenshot.
