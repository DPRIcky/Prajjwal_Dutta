01Background

I keep robot teams together when the world pushes them apart.

The swarm drifting behind this page is the problem I spend my days on. The agents are carried along by a flow field, and they have to keep their communication graph intact without running into each other. At ASU's AMASS Lab, with funding from NSF and NIWC, I work on decentralized control that keeps robot fleets connected when the environment won't sit still. That work has taken me from AUVs in ocean currents to aerial platforms I designed, built and flew myself, because I wanted to see the math survive outside simulation. I started with a BTech in Electronics and Communication at VIT Vellore, finished my M.S. in Robotics and Autonomous Systems at ASU with a thesis in 2025, and I'm wrapping up a second M.S. in Manufacturing Engineering in December 2026.

Current Research · Underwater Robot Fleets · IROS 2026AMASS Lab · NSF

Keeping Flow-Driven Robot Fleets Connected on Six-Byte Messages

My main research, and my first-author IROS 2026 paper. Underwater robots drift apart with the current and talk over short, lossy acoustic links, so I split the problem in two. A distributed δ-BFS protocol trims the communication graph down to a spanning tree using constant six-byte broadcasts, and a CLF-CBF-QP safety filter keeps only those tree links in range while the robots avoid each other and head for a goal. Compared with adjacency consensus it builds the tree just as reliably, far faster and with a fraction of the traffic, and I tested the whole pipeline with BlueROV2 fleets in HoloOcean under currents and a channel made bad on purpose.

Decentralized Multi-Robot Coordination Under Degraded Comms

I designed a decentralized coordination system that scales, pairing graph pruning with CLF-CBF-QP safety constraints. Alongside it I built a warehouse navigation stack and put A*, Hybrid A* and RRT* up against MPC, LQR, Pure Pursuit, Stanley and PID on an AgileX Limo, with motion capture as ground truth. To test the connectivity law somewhere less forgiving than a simulator, I designed and built a custom aerial platform and flew it, and the behavior carried over from sim to the real thing.

Industrial Automation · Multi-Robot Cell · 2026RAS 598 / RS4

PLC-Coordinated Semiconductor Wafer Handling Cell

A four-axis SCARA, a six-DoF cobot, a stepper conveyor with Hough-circle inspection and an Allen-Bradley Micro820, all passing 100 mm wafers from a stacked cassette to a deposit platform, one shelf lower each cycle. I moved the extraction stage onto a new arm whose API has no arm-configuration selector. That turned into a real problem at the deposit fixture, which sits at 393.8 mm of a 400 mm reach where only one elbow branch stays inside the joint limits, so I solved it with a mix of Cartesian and joint-space moves. I also checked the seven rungs actually running on the PLC against the manual's out-of-date listing, and designed the two-wire handshake so every signal fails in a direction I chose.

Safety-Critical Control · Fall 2025EGR 611

CLF-CBF-QP Safety Filtering for 70-Robot Fleets

A safety filter, solved as a quadratic program, that keeps 70 robots from colliding and keeps them in line of sight of each other through clutter and vortex flow fields. I gave quadprog, CVX and OSQP exactly the same QP instances to see which one held up, and read the KKT multipliers to find out which constraint was actually steering the fleet at each moment.

Robust Vehicle Control · Spring 2026EGR 560

Tube-MPC Lateral Path Tracking in CarSim

Lateral control for an automated car that has to stay on its line when things go wrong. A nominal MPC plans the center of the tube, a discrete LQR loop pushes back against disturbances, and actuator-aware scaling and saturation keep the steering commands physically possible. In Simulink the worst lateral error dropped from 18.80 m to 0.032 m, and in the full CarSim co-simulation at 54 km/h the car never drifted more than 0.29 m.

Aerial Autonomy · Hardware · Spring 2025RAS 598

Autonomous Blimp, from Custom Airframe to Vision Autonomy

A lighter-than-air robot we built from scratch. The airframe was designed in SolidWorks and fabricated in house, with a custom integration PCB laid out in Altium for the ESC, IMU, barometer and JST power, and the hardware brought up on an oscilloscope and logic analyzer. The ROS 2 stack fuses a 200 Hz IMU with a 30 fps camera through a Kalman filter, and YOLOv5 detections steer the blimp. It became our hardware testbed for multi-agent sim-to-real work.
